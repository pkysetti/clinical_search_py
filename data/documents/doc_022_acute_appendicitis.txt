DOCUMENT TYPE: Emergency Department Note  |  DEPARTMENT: General Surgery

Marcus T. is a 24-year-old male with no significant past medical history who was brought to the emergency department by his roommate after approximately 18 hours of progressive abdominal pain. He reports that the pain began as a diffuse, periumbilical ache yesterday afternoon and has since migrated and localized to the right lower quadrant, where it is now sharp and constant, rating 8 out of 10 in severity. He has experienced nausea with two episodes of vomiting since this morning and notes a loss of appetite. He had one loose bowel movement yesterday but denies diarrhea today. He denies dysuria, hematuria, or any urethral discharge. He has no prior abdominal surgeries and takes no regular medications. Family history is unremarkable. He does not smoke, drinks alcohol socially, and denies any illicit drug use.

On presentation, his vital signs are notable for a temperature of 38.4°C, blood pressure 124/78 mmHg, heart rate 102 beats per minute, and oxygen saturation 99% on room air. He appears uncomfortable and is lying still on the gurney. Abdominal examination reveals marked tenderness at McBurney's point in the right lower quadrant with voluntary guarding. Rebound tenderness is present. Rovsing's sign is positive. Psoas and obturator signs are equivocal. The remainder of the abdomen is soft without organomegaly. Complete blood count shows a white blood cell count of 14,800 cells/µL with 88% neutrophils and a left shift, consistent with a bacterial inflammatory process. C-reactive protein is elevated at 42 mg/L. Urinalysis is unremarkable with no pyuria or bacteriuria, making urinary tract infection or urolithiasis less likely. A contrast-enhanced CT scan of the abdomen and pelvis was obtained and demonstrates a dilated, non-compressible appendix measuring 9 mm in diameter with periappendiceal fat stranding and no evidence of free perforation or abscess formation, findings diagnostic of acute uncomplicated appendicitis. No alternative cause of the patient's symptoms is identified on imaging.

The patient was diagnosed with acute uncomplicated appendicitis and consented for laparoscopic appendectomy after a thorough discussion of risks, benefits, and alternatives including non-operative antibiotic management, which he declined in favor of surgical intervention. He was made nil per os, started on intravenous cefoxitin for perioperative prophylaxis, and received intravenous morphine for pain control with good effect. He was taken to the operating room the same evening and underwent an uncomplicated laparoscopic appendectomy under general anesthesia. Intraoperative findings confirmed an acutely inflamed appendix with no perforation, gangrenous change, or free fluid in the peritoneal cavity. The specimen was sent to pathology. Postoperative recovery in the PACU was uneventful. He was tolerating clear liquids by the following morning and was discharged home on postoperative day one with instructions for wound care, activity restrictions, and a course of oral cephalexin. He is to follow up with the general surgery clinic in two weeks.
