The clinical presentation of palpitations, heat intolerance, weight loss, tremor, tachycardia, exophthalmos, and markedly abnormal thyroid function with positive TRAb establishes the diagnosis of Graves' disease with hyperthyroidism. This is a newly identified diagnosis explaining many of the patient's somatic symptoms. Critically, hyperthyroidism is a well-recognized cause of anxiety, emotional lability, insomnia, and mood disturbance that can mimic or exacerbate underlying depression and GAD.

Endocrinology consulted on day 1. Anti-thyroid medication initiated: methimazole 30mg daily (titrated to effect). Propranolol 20mg three times daily started for symptomatic control of tachycardia, tremor, and anxiety (beta-blockade blocks peripheral conversion of T4 to T3 and provides symptomatic relief). The psychiatric team noted that a significant proportion of the patient's current psychiatric symptoms (anxiety, emotional lability, agitation, sleep disruption) are likely attributable to or exacerbated by the uncontrolled hyperthyroidism.
